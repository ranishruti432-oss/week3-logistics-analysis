This report presents an end-to-end exploratory data analysis and visualization project for a hypothetical logistics operation. A reproducible dataset of 300 simulated shipments was created, covering shipment date, region, transportation mode, route distance, shipment volume, delivery time, transportation cost, weather-related delay and on-time status. The report states clearly that the data is synthetic, so the findings demonstrate an analytical workflow and not the performance of a real company.

The analysis is done in Python using Pandas and NumPy for preparation and calculations, and Matplotlib and Seaborn for visualization. The exploratory work includes data-quality checks (missing values, duplicate IDs, invalid distances and costs), descriptive statistics for central tendency and spread, summaries by transport mode and region, and a Pearson correlation matrix. The report includes eleven visualizations: a delivery-time histogram, a box plot of delivery time by mode, a distance-versus-cost scatter plot, a regional delivery-time bar chart, a correlation heatmap, a cost-by-mode bar chart, a monthly shipment-volume line chart, an on-time rate chart by region, a volume-versus-delivery-time scatter plot, a region-by-mode heatmap, and a Seaborn scatter plot with trend lines that explains why air shipments form a separate, higher cost band. Every chart comes with a reason for choosing that chart type and an interpretation that links it to a logistics decision.

The key findings cover the overall on-time rate under a clearly stated three-day threshold (50.67%), the slower East region, the cost and speed trade-off between Road, Rail and Air, and the strong link between distance and cost. Additional analysis covers KPI definitions, cost per kilometre and per kilogram, IQR outlier detection (with a note on why it does not suit the zero-inflated weather-delay variable), monthly volume monitoring and a region-by-mode comparison. Recommendations focus on service-level tracking, investigating the East region, choosing transport mode lane by lane, using normalized cost metrics, regular outlier review and collecting richer operational data. Limitations are documented, including the simulated nature of the data and the fact that correlation is not causation. The Python script and CSV files let anyone reproduce and adapt the analysis for real logistics data.
